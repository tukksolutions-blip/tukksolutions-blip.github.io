FAQ

Questions, answered straight

The things homeowners in Bolton and Caledon ask us most — no jargon, no sales pitch. Still unsure? Call (905) 783-7029 and ask us directly.

How often should my chimney be swept?+
Once a year is the standard recommendation — and it's what insurance providers and WETT guidelines expect. If you burn more than two or three cords of wood a season, a mid-season check is worth it. Creosote builds up with every fire, and a sweep is far cheaper than a chimney fire.
What is a WETT inspection, and when do I need one?+
WETT stands for Wood Energy Technology Transfer — Canada's standard for wood-burning systems. A certified WETT inspection assesses whether your stove, fireplace and chimney are installed and operating safely and to code. You'll typically need one when buying or selling a home, when your insurer asks for it, or after installing a new wood-burning appliance. We provide a written report you can hand straight to your insurer or lawyer.
How long does a chimney sweep take, and will it make a mess?+
A standard sweep takes about 45 to 60 minutes. We seal the fireplace opening, use professional vacuum equipment, and protect your floors and furniture — the soot goes into our system, not your living room. You can use your fireplace again the same evening.
What is creosote, and why is it dangerous?+
Creosote is the tar-like residue that builds up inside your flue every time wood burns. It's highly flammable — a hot fire can ignite it, causing a chimney fire that can crack the liner and spread to the structure of your home. Regular sweeping removes it before it reaches dangerous levels, and it's the main reason annual sweeping matters.
What are the signs my chimney needs repair?+
Watch for white staining on the brick (efflorescence — a sign of moisture), crumbling or missing mortar, rust on the damper or firebox, water stains on walls or ceilings near the chimney, and pieces of tile in the fireplace. Any of these means it's time for an inspection before the damage spreads.
Do you install wood stoves and fireplace inserts?+
Yes — we supply and install wood stoves and inserts, and we make sure the installation meets code and passes its WETT inspection. If you're upgrading an open fireplace, an insert is one of the best efficiency improvements you can make: far more heat into the room, far less up the chimney.

How much does a chimney sweep cost?+
It depends on the number of flues, the condition of the chimney and how much creosote has built up — which is why we quote exactly rather than guessing.
